# AquaSense — marketing and business website analysis

Pilot accessed 2026-10-27. Three directly accessible official companies: Innovasea, AKVA group and RYNAN. Canonical records: [A01–A07](2026-10-27_sources.md). Facts below describe inspected pages; recommendations and buyer-role interpretations are PROJECT_ASSUMPTION, not established AquaSense capabilities.

Common messages connect environmental visibility to farm decisions, operational consistency and resource use. This is broader than displaying sensor values. Product detail explains what is monitored; business copy explains why operators should care. Prices were not collected from these pages; contact routes support a considered purchase, but exact subscription, hardware and implementation terms remain unknown.

## Buyer interpretation and business value

Proposed primary buyers are farm owners and operations managers responsible for production risk and spending. Daily users may be farm supervisors and water-quality technicians. Investors and integrators are secondary audiences. These roles are inferred from solution language and forms, not interview evidence.

The strongest landing-page hypothesis is earlier awareness of water-condition changes with clearer actions for farm staff. Secondary value hypotheses are less manual reporting and better visibility across ponds. Revenue gains, survival improvements, lower feed conversion ratios and energy savings need AquaSense-specific measurements. Innovasea's salmon biomass case is relevant as a proof-writing pattern; its results cannot substantiate AquaSense pond monitoring.

## Proposed landing-page sections

1. Outcome-led hero naming the farm type and the decision AquaSense supports. Primary proposed CTA: “Discuss your farm”; secondary: “See a sample monitoring workflow.”
2. Customer problems: overnight changes, scattered checks and delayed escalation. Validate these with local farmers before presenting them as universal.
3. Benefits linked to observable workflows: monitor, alert, investigate and document. Distinguish available functionality from roadmap items.
4. Water-quality coverage and deployment: verified sensor parameters, installation, connectivity, maintenance and response responsibilities.
5. A short business scenario showing who receives an alert and what they do next; dashboard visuals support this explanation.
6. Evidence: a real pilot with dates, farm context, baseline, measurement method and limitations. Until available, label demonstrations clearly.
7. Commercial approach: what hardware, service, installation and support include. Publish prices only after internal approval.
8. FAQ, service coverage, contact details and a repeated consultation CTA.

Use direct, practical language and farm-specific examples. Avoid unsupported “best”, guaranteed productivity or quantified sustainability promises. Reuse the general sequence of problem, benefit, proof and contact without copying competitor brand assets, wording or distinctive layouts.
