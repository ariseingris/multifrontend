## Product and case-study evidence

[DJI urban-fire content](https://enterprise.dji.com/public-safety/urban-fires) positions thermal reconnaissance for assessment and coordination. [Elios 3](https://www.flyability.com/elios-3) describes visual/thermal inspection and GPS-denied operation. These are vendor capability statements; this pilot did not independently test aircraft or payloads.

DJI's homepage links wildfire and volcanic-response case-study videos. Their presence is a trust-building observation; no measured rescue outcome was collected from the videos. Flyability's maximum cost-reduction statement is not a documented causal result for an emergency-response mission. CT UAV's [homepage](https://ctuav.global/en/) distinguishes fire support from emergency delivery; promotional technology, export and certification assertions were not independently verified or adopted.

The legacy [FLIR UAS firefighting page](https://www.flir.ca/suas/uas-for-firefighting/) returned 404. The accessible [firefighting-camera category](https://www.flir.com/browse/first-responders/firefighting-cameras/) supports a narrower thermal-equipment comparison. It does not establish current FLIR UAV availability or specifications.

## Market and financial interpretation

NIFC gives incident context for a specific national market. Neither acres nor fire counts measure emergency-services procurement, drone adoption, FireScout revenue opportunity or lives saved. No TAM, CAGR, currency loss estimate or revenue forecast was calculated. The completed 2025 row was preferred to a changing 2026 year-to-date number.
