## Buyer interpretation and value

Proposed primary buyers are fire-service leadership, incident commanders and public-safety procurement. Drone program managers and pilots evaluate deployment, payloads, maintenance and operating limits. Industrial emergency teams are a potential secondary segment. These roles require customer validation in the target geography.

The strongest FireScout hypothesis is clearer incident awareness and coordination using aerial and thermal information. Keep reconnaissance, search support, delivery and active suppression as separate use cases with separate proof. An inspection drone's cost claim does not establish rescue performance, and a thermal image does not establish AI detection accuracy.

## Proposed landing-page sections

1. Mission-focused hero describing the actual supported response task; proposed primary CTA: “Discuss a response scenario.”
2. Operational problems: incomplete scene information and coordination delays, validated with responders.
3. Use-case paths for fire reconnaissance, thermal inspection and search support, restricted to demonstrated capabilities.
4. Workflow: prepare, deploy, observe, communicate and review. Explain responsibilities in plain language.
5. Customer-relevant capabilities: payload, image quality, connectivity, endurance conditions and environmental limits, using verified project specifications.
6. Evidence: dated, documented field exercises with scenario, operators, baseline, measured results and limitations. Label simulation footage and sample telemetry clearly.
7. Deployment and support: training, maintenance, integration and service availability. Address procurement questions without unverified approvals or certification badges.
8. FAQ and commercial consultation CTA; optional secondary CTA to view a clearly labeled demonstration.

Use clear mission language and short explanations. Avoid guarantees about lives saved, flight safety, extinguishing capability or AI precision. No competitor assets or proprietary layouts should be reused.
